Dear Editor,

I am submitting "Timer Lawn: Solving the Timer Wheel Overflow Problem for Large Scale and High Throughput Systems" for your consideration.

I have spent the last decade working in high-performance and large-scale systems: as a research assistant and later an associate researcher in the MOSIX preemptive process migration lab at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, at a government-operated research center, and since then as a private consultant and engineer with Tamar Labs, Redis Labs, and NextSilicon, where I currently work.

This paper presents Lawn, a timer data structure that maps queues by time-to-live rather than by absolute expiration time. That single change gives it an unbounded TTL span with no rebuild-on-overflow cost, the failure mode that limits the standard timing wheel in systems where the maximum TTL is unpredictable or the tick resolution needs to change. The TTL-keyed queue-map idea and the name were introduced in an earlier preprint of mine. This submission is a substantial extension of that work: an allocation-free implementation (Lawn2), a differential correctness gate validated across four independently written implementations, a directly measured crossover map showing exactly where Lawn's advantage holds and where it does not, and new results the earlier version did not report, including memory behavior at scale, workload characterization, and a robustness sweep to hundreds of millions of timers. I state the trade-off's boundary quantitatively rather than qualitatively, and report plainly where Lawn should and should not be used.

Lawn is already in production use by several organizations, some independent of me, including Redis Internals for streams and other internal timers, the ReDe Redis dehydration module, Mellanox RDMA timers for Unified Communication X, and an independent clib timer-management utility library. Source is available for the ReDe and Unified Communication X implementations.

Thank you for considering this paper. I am glad to answer any questions you or the reviewers may have.
